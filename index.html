How this page reads ALLW

What the fund is. Bridgewater's All Weather portfolio run as an ETF: long-only risk parity, aiming for roughly 10 to 12% volatility. Bridgewater sends a model portfolio each day and State Street trades it. Positions do not flip from long to short. What changes is how big each sleeve is and how much leverage the whole book carries.

Exposure. Every figure is a share of the fund's total net assets on the same date, from State Street's NAV history. Futures and the commodity swap count at their notional value. In the holdings file their Weight column is margin or profit and loss, not exposure, so it is not used. Everything held outright (TIPS, equity ETFs) counts at market value.

Sleeves. Four risk sleeves: equities (ETFs plus index futures), nominal government bonds (all futures), inflation-linked bonds (US TIPS held directly) and commodities (gold futures plus a total return swap on the Bloomberg Commodity index). Cash and collateral (a government money fund, T-bills, dollar and other currency balances) backs the futures and is shown beside the sleeves, not counted as exposure.

Gross and leverage. Gross exposure is the four risk sleeves added up. Leverage is gross over 100%: 1.9x means each dollar in the fund carries about $1.90 of market exposure.

Balance. The share of gross is a share of NOTIONAL. Risk parity balances risk, and a bond dollar carries much less risk than an equity dollar, so in notional terms the book always looks bond heavy. A move in the shares is the signal, not their level.

Performance. Total returns as State Street publishes them on the fund's page, at the last month end and the last quarter end, beside the benchmark the fund names (MSCI ACWI IMI, an all-equity index, while the fund holds well under half its exposure in equities). Market price is the NAV times one plus State Street's published premium or discount for the same day.

History. State Street keeps only the latest holdings file online, so the daily history starts the day archiving began and grows by one file per business day. The file can trail the market by up to two business days. Before that, the book comes from two public records: the fund's quarterly SEC filings (Form N-PORT, every position at each fiscal quarter end in January, April, July and October, public about 60 days later), and the few copies of the holdings file the Internet Archive saved, launch day among them. State Street's asset class exposure sheet adds month ends to the chart: its own totals for the four sleeves and gross, with no cash line and no market detail, so those dates appear only in the chart. On 30 April 2026, the one date both cover, it is within 1.7 points of the SEC filing in every sleeve. The 2025 copies do not give the commodity swap's size, so commodities and gross are left out on those dates. Comparisons with a week, month, quarter or year ago use the nearest book to that date and name it. The NAV history goes back to the fund's launch on 5 March 2025.
